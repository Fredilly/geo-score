# GEO Score — Product Brief

## Purpose

Build a premium, minimalist website diagnostic product for Article6.

The product should do one thing extremely well:

> Enter a website URL → analyze it → return a clear score → explain the biggest problems → convert the visitor into a GEO Website Upgrade lead.

This is not a generic agency website.

The analyzer is the homepage and the product.

## Core Product Idea

### Primary question

**Can AI understand your website?**

The user enters a URL and receives an Article6-defined website readiness score.

The experience should feel like a modern technical diagnostic product, not an SEO report.

Think: premium, minimal, interactive, high-trust, fast, data-led, visually technical without overwhelming the user.

Avoid: generic agency sections, long marketing copy, AI robot imagery, purple SaaS gradients, SEO jargon, giant audit reports, fake precision, and guaranteed rankings or AI citations.

## Primary User Flow

### 1. Landing State

Full-screen hero.

**Can AI understand your website?**

Enter your website. We’ll measure it.

Input:

`https://yourwebsite.com`

CTA:

**Run analysis →**

The URL input is the main visual and functional element.

### 2. Analysis State

After submission, transition into a short animated analysis experience.

Suggested stages:

1. Reading site
2. Checking access
3. Understanding the business
4. Testing answerability
5. Measuring trust
6. Calculating score

Keep the language simple. Do not expose the full technical audit during this stage.

Visual metaphor: **ambiguity → clarity**

Possible motion:
- blurred content becoming sharp
- disconnected nodes resolving into relationships
- score bars forming
- signal lines connecting
- subtle data grids
- structured blocks snapping into place

### 3. Score State

Display one dominant score:

**67 / 100**

Example interpretation:

**Your site is visible.  
It is not easy to understand.**

The score must be clearly identified as an Article6-defined diagnostic score.

It must never be presented as an official Google, Bing, OpenAI, ChatGPT, Perplexity, or third-party score.

## User-Facing Score Categories

The internal scoring system can remain detailed, but the public product should simplify it.

### Access
Can search and AI systems crawl and read the site?

### Understanding
Can systems clearly identify the business, services, people, market, and purpose?

### Answers
Does the site directly answer important customer questions?

### Trust
Are claims, expertise, contact details, evidence, and business identity clear and supportable?

### Authority
Is the site specific, differentiated, and useful enough to stand apart from generic content?

Example:
- Access: 91
- Understanding: 54
- Answers: 61
- Trust: 42
- Authority: 70

## Internal Scoring Source

Use the existing Article6 GEO rubric as the scoring foundation.

Internal categories:

1. Crawlability and Indexability
2. Technical Structure
3. Structured Data
4. Entity Clarity
5. Answerability and Information Architecture
6. Trust and Evidence
7. Content Differentiation

Total: 100 points.

Every awarded point must be backed by observable evidence.

Do not inflate scores to improve conversion.

## Results Language

Results should be concise and outcome-focused.

Good:

**Understanding · 54**

AI can see what you do.  
It cannot confidently identify who you are.

**Answers · 61**

Important customer questions are buried instead of answered directly.

**Trust · 42**

Your claims are difficult for machines to verify.

**Access · 91**

Your site is technically accessible.

Avoid long technical explanations, unexplained schema terminology, crawl-budget jargon, and developer-focused error dumps.

Technical evidence can exist behind expandable details later.

## Free Diagnostic Boundary

Do not give away the full paid audit.

The free experience should provide:

- overall score
- category scores
- top 3 issues
- short explanation of each
- estimated number of additional opportunities
- clear next action

Example:

**58 / 100**

3 critical issues  
7 additional opportunities

Then reveal only the three highest-value findings.

The detailed remediation plan belongs to the paid service.

## Commercial Transition

The site should naturally move from diagnosis to outcome.

Suggested transition:

**This is not really a technology problem.**

When machines cannot clearly understand your company, they are less able to retrieve it, describe it, recommend it, or cite it.

Then:

## We fix what the score finds.

**Audit → Implement → Verify**

The paid service is the existing Article6 GEO Website Upgrade.

Do not expose internal delivery economics unless intentionally used in the final sales design.

## Paid Outcome

Describe the paid service through outcomes, not implementation jargon.

Focus on:

- clearer machine-readable business identity
- stronger search and AI discoverability foundations
- better structured data
- better answerability
- cleaner technical indexing setup
- stronger trust and evidence signals
- before/after verification

Do not promise guaranteed rankings, guaranteed ChatGPT citations, guaranteed AI recommendations, guaranteed traffic, guaranteed leads, or guaranteed revenue.

## Before / After Mechanic

A major visual element can show improvement.

Example:

**67 → 91**

### Before

Professional solutions for businesses worldwide.

### After

Clear company.  
Clear services.  
Clear expertise.  
Clear evidence.  
Clear answers.

The product should make improvement feel measurable and tangible.

## Primary CTA

Only one primary CTA should dominate each state.

Landing:

**Run analysis →**

Results:

**Improve my score →**

The results CTA should lead to a short qualification/contact flow.

Avoid multiple competing CTAs.

## Visual Direction

### Overall

Premium technical minimalism.

### Palette

Base:
- near-black / graphite
- white / near-white

Primary accent:
- electric blue

Status accents:
- restrained green for strong/improved
- amber for caution
- red only for genuinely serious issues

Avoid overusing status colors.

### Typography

- large editorial headlines
- restrained supporting copy
- strong numeric typography
- generous spacing
- high contrast
- minimal visual noise

### UI

Use:
- thin borders
- subtle glass effects
- restrained gradients
- soft glow only around live analysis/data
- clean data visualization
- strong score presentation
- large interactive URL field

Do not build a dashboard-heavy interface.

## Brand Behavior

The score itself should become a recognizable Article6 product asset.

The experience should feel more like high-end developer infrastructure and less like an SEO agency, marketing funnel, or AI chatbot landing page.

## Technical Product Principles

### Fast first impression

The homepage must load quickly.

Do not sacrifice speed for decorative animation.

### Progressive analysis

The user should immediately see feedback after submitting a URL.

If full analysis takes time, surface meaningful progress rather than a static spinner.

### Failure states

Handle:
- malformed URL
- unreachable website
- blocked crawling
- timeout
- redirects
- unsupported site
- partial analysis

Never return a fake score when evidence is insufficient.

Example:

**We could only complete part of the analysis.**

Show what was measured and clearly mark unavailable sections.

## Evidence Rules

The score must remain defensible.

The system should never:

- invent company facts
- infer credentials
- fabricate trust signals
- treat unavailable measurements as zero without justification
- claim external AI visibility changes without evidence
- present manual AI tests as deterministic rankings

The scoring product measures website readiness.

External outcomes such as actual AI citations, rankings, traffic, or conversions remain separate measurements.

## Product Architecture

The first version only needs:

1. Homepage analyzer
2. Analysis/loading state
3. Results state
4. Lead capture / contact flow
5. Basic privacy/legal pages
6. Analytics
7. Error handling

Do not add a blog, resources hub, client portal, account system, complex dashboard, CMS, large navigation, or unrelated Article6 services unless necessary later.

## Conversion Principle

The website should demonstrate competence before asking the user to trust Article6.

The sales sequence is:

**Experience the diagnostic → see the problem → understand the consequence → see that Article6 fixes it.**

The product itself is the sales proof.

## Product Guardrails

1. The analyzer is the homepage.
2. One dominant action at a time.
3. No generic agency homepage.
4. No bloated navigation.
5. No technical jargon unless progressively disclosed.
6. No fake score.
7. No fabricated findings.
8. No guaranteed ranking/citation outcomes.
9. Free analysis reveals enough to create conviction, not enough to replace the paid service.
10. Preserve the existing Article6 audit, scoring, implementation, QA, and before/after methodology.
11. Premium design must not reduce usability or speed.
12. Mobile experience is first-class.

## Success Criteria

A first-time visitor should understand within seconds:

1. What to do: **Enter my website.**
2. What they receive: **A clear readiness score.**
3. Why it matters: **AI and search systems may struggle to understand and surface the site.**
4. What Article6 does: **Find the weaknesses, fix them, and verify the improvement.**

Anything that does not support those four points should be questioned before it is added.
